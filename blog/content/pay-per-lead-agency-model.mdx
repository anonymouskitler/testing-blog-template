A pay per lead agency charges a fixed fee for each lead it delivers that meets a written definition, instead of a monthly retainer. You pay nothing for clicks or impressions. But you carry every cost after delivery, so the lead definition, exclusivity and returns terms in the contract set your real price.

## Key takeaways

- Pay per lead means a fixed fee per lead that meets an agreed definition. You pay for the record, not for a sale.
- Three clauses set your real price: what counts as a lead, whether it is exclusive, and which leads you can return.
- Published prices span a huge range because "lead" means different things. A form fill and a booked meeting can differ by 10x.
- Judge the deal by cost per sale, not cost per lead.
- The agency earns more by sending more leads. Quality only holds if the contract and your weekly review enforce it.

## How does a pay per lead agency work?

A pay per lead agency finds people who want a service, captures their details, checks them against your criteria and bills you for each one that passes. The agency pays for the traffic and takes the campaign risk. You pay for results that meet the definition.

[Lead Distro AI describes](https://www.leaddistro.ai/blog/what-is-pay-per-lead) the flow as three steps: generate, qualify, deliver. The agency runs ads or content to get a form fill or a call. It then removes duplicates, bad contact data and out-of-area people. Leads reach you by webhook, API, email or CRM.

[SyncGTM adds a fourth step](https://syncgtm.com/blog/what-is-pay-per-lead): acceptance. You usually get a window of 24 to 72 hours to accept or reject each lead. Rejections need a reason, such as a wrong job title, a duplicate or a dead phone number.

## What does a pay per lead agency charge?

Prices run from about $10 for a shared consumer lead to several thousand dollars for a sales-qualified B2B opportunity. No single average is reliable, because each source counts a different event as a "lead".

[Cuefully compared three public datasets](https://cuefully.com/blog/average-cost-per-lead-by-industry) and found published averages from $27 to $3,080. One counts a search-ad conversion, one a blended marketing lead, and one a lead that sales already qualified.

## Which three contract clauses set your real price?

Your real price is set by the lead definition, exclusivity and the returns policy. A low headline price with weak terms in these three places costs more per sale than a higher price with strong ones.

**The lead definition.** Get in writing what counts: a raw form fill, a verified contact, a qualified lead or an inbound call. [Ripe Leads notes](https://ripeleads.eu/resources/pay-per-lead-vs-retainer) that "a lead, a qualified lead and a positive reply are three different things" and that vendors use the terms interchangeably. Spell out company size, location, sector, job title and the exact action that shows interest.

**Exclusivity.** An exclusive lead goes to one buyer. A shared lead goes to several at once. [LeadsNow suggests](https://leadsnow.ai/pay-per-lead/) a rough rule: a lead shared with N buyers is worth about 1/N of an exclusive one to you, unless you reliably reach the person first. They label it a decision rule, not a measured law. Test it against your own results.

**The returns policy.** Ask which leads you can send back, on what evidence, and within how many days. Ask whether you get a credit, a refund or a replacement. Without a returns clause you pay for invalid leads as well as good ones.

## What should you expect from a pay per lead agency?

Expect volume, speed and a reporting trail. Do not expect closed sales, and do not expect the agency to follow up for you. The agency's revenue rises with each lead that clears your definition, so its incentive is more leads.

Reasonable expectations to hold the agency to:

- A written lead definition and a sample of what a passing lead looks like.
- Delivery within minutes. The [Harvard Business Review lead response study](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), cited by Lead Distro AI, found that leads contacted within five minutes were far more likely to qualify than leads contacted after 30 minutes.
- Proof of consent where the law requires it, and duplicate checks against your CRM.
- A named process for rejecting leads, with a deadline and a limit on how many rejections trigger a talk about the contract.
- Source reporting, so you can see which channel each lead came from.

What you carry yourself: contacting each lead, qualifying them, booking, and closing. Your sales team's speed and skill change the result as much as the agency's work does.

## Is pay per lead cheaper than a retainer?

Pay per lead is cheaper per lead only when a lead is tightly defined, easy to verify and worth about the same each time. For considered B2B sales, a retainer or a hybrid often gives better leads for the same money.

[Launchleads recommends](https://www.launchleads.com/buyers-guides/pricing-models-explained/) pay per lead for ungated content offers with tight definitions, and a hybrid for new relationships: 70 to 80 percent retainer and 20 to 30 percent variable pay tied to qualified meetings above a floor. [Leadriver reports](https://www.leadriver.io/blog/lead-generation-pricing-models-explained) that pay per lead pushes agencies toward the cheapest channels, usually high-volume cold email, and that it works only with strict criteria, weekly quality review and a clawback clause.

| Model | You pay for | Who carries the risk | Fits best |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pay per lead | Each lead that meets the definition | Agency on delivery, you on quality | High-volume, low-value, easy-to-verify demand |
| Pay per meeting | Each booked meeting | Shared, but no-shows are common | Predictable sales motions |
| Retainer | Agency time and scope | You, during the ramp-up | Considered B2B sales |
| Hybrid | Base fee plus a bonus per outcome | Shared | New agency relationships |

## How do you check that a lead price is worth paying?

Divide the cost of your leads by your close rate to get cost per sale, then compare it with the gross profit on a sale. If cost per sale is not comfortably below gross profit, the lead price is too high, however cheap it looks.

LeadsNow gives an example: at $60 a lead and a 5 percent lead-to-sale rate, each sale costs $1,200 in leads alone. Add your team's time spent calling and booking, which most buyers leave out. [Leadgen Economy warns](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/cost-per-lead-cpl-benchmarks-industry/) that true cost per lead runs 30 to 60 percent above the dashboard number once returns, compliance and labor are added.

Run a small test first:

1. Agree a pilot of 30 to 50 leads with the written definition.
2. Track every lead through contact, appointment and sale.
3. Reject leads that break the definition inside the window, with reasons.
4. Compare cost per sale with gross profit per sale.
5. Scale only if the numbers hold. Renegotiate the definition if they do not.

## What goes wrong, and how do you prevent it?

The common failure is leads that technically pass the definition but never close. It happens when the definition is vague, the agency reaches for cheap traffic, or nobody reviews quality.

Prevent it with these terms:

- A monthly cap on leads, so volume cannot grow past what your team can work.
- Weekly lead review in the first quarter, not an end-of-month surprise.
- A rejection window of days, not weeks, with a credit on rejected leads.
- Exclusivity or a maximum number of buyers per lead.
- An exit clause if close rates sit below the normal range for your field for two months running.

## FAQ

### What is a pay per lead agency?

A pay per lead agency is a marketing company that bills you a fixed fee for each lead that meets an agreed definition, instead of charging a monthly retainer. It runs the ads or content, captures the lead and passes it to you. You handle follow-up and sales.

### How much does pay per lead cost?

It depends on what counts as a lead. Reported prices run from $10 to $25 for shared consumer leads, $200 to $600 for exclusive personal injury leads, and $300 to $800 for booked B2B meetings. Compare any quote against cost per sale, not another vendor's per-lead price.

### Do I pay for bad leads?

You pay for them unless the contract says otherwise. Ask for a returns clause that names the reasons you can reject a lead, the evidence needed, the deadline, and whether you get a credit, a refund or a replacement.

### Is pay per lead better than pay per meeting?

Not always. A meeting is harder to fake than a lead and sits closer to pipeline, but pay per meeting has its own problems, including no-shows and meetings with people who cannot buy. Both need a written qualification standard.

### Who is pay per lead right for?

It suits businesses with a narrow target customer, a clear lead definition, and a sales team that contacts leads within minutes. It suits them less when the sale is complex and one good conversation matters more than twenty weak ones.
